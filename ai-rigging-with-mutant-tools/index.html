AI rigging an orc with Mutant Tools

A purchased orc with no joints, rigged end to end by Claude driving Maya through scripts. The body comes from the Zombinator, the face from Meta Mutant, and the rig is then tested on dynamic poses, expressions and a face performance taken from a video.

  • MAYA
  • MUTANT TOOLS
  • ZOMBINATOR
  • META MUTANT
  • CLAUDE

01The idea

Nobody paints weights or sculpts shapes here. Both halves of the rig are borrowed from a human that is already rigged.

Body: the Zombinator base mesh, with its guides and skin weights.

Face: a MetaHuman head, with its joints, corrective shapes and control board.

In both cases the carrier is a wrap: the human mesh reshaped until it lies on the orc. Guides, weights, joints and shapes all ride on that mesh, so a sloppy wrap gives a sloppy rig. Most of the work is getting the two wraps right, and then proving the result.

Claude wrote and ran every step as a Python script in Maya, rendered the result, looked at it and measured it before moving on.

02The model

The model is Orc Gralnok, bought on CGTrader: six meshes in an A-pose, no joints and no textures. The import removes the keyed transforms, names the meshes and gives them flat colours.

03Wrap the body

TopoTransfer fits the Zombinator base mesh onto the orc. It needs pairs of matching points on both characters, and on a body this far from a human those pairs decide everything.

Limbs: from the same spot along a bone on both characters, rays go out to the skin. Each hit is one pair. A ray that leaves the limb and lands on the trunk is dropped.

Trunk: nipples, navel, sternal notch and the pec line, picked by eye. The orc’s ribcage is much longer than a human’s, so rings at spine-joint heights put the belly on his pecs.

Then relax and project. Each vertex slides along the orc’s skin towards the middle of its neighbours and snaps back onto the surface. That spreads the mesh evenly, so the borrowed weights land on the right muscles.

04Guides, build and skin

The guides come with the wrap. Each limb guide is checked against where it sits in the human mesh, not where it looks tidy: a borrowed skin wants the pivot it was painted for. The elbow had to move 4.5 cm.

The Auto Rigger builds the Human Body Template, and the weights are copied from the wrapped mesh to the orc. Two things the copy cannot do on its own:

Shoulders

The top of the deltoid still belonged to the clavicle. A vertex selection around the shoulder joint moves most of that weight to the shoulder and fades out towards the neck. With the arms up, crushed triangles went from 59 to 2.

Twists

Limbs use dual quaternion skinning where the twist joints carry them, and the twist weights are an even ramp along the bone. A forearm twisted 85° keeps its full thickness, where linear skinning shrank it to 89.5 %.

05Test the body

First a range of motion: 29 single-joint poses, each measured for volume and crushed triangles.

Then eight dynamic poses, the way an animator would push the rig. Each is built on one line of action, and the camera is the view where the silhouette reads best. Planted feet stay on the floor and fists close with the thumb wrapped.

06Meta Mutant: wrap the head

Meta Mutant reshapes a MetaHuman head to yours and builds the face rig from it. Same rule as the body: the rig is only as good as that wrap.

30 points and six lines along the lips and eyelids are picked on both heads. TopoTransfer runs four passes, then the skin is relaxed and projected onto the orc.

Three places need their own rule:

Eyes: the orc’s lid edge is buried inside the eyeball, so the lid margin is walked out to the line where lid and eye really meet.

Mouth: he has an underbite, so the inner lips are walked onto his own, upper on upper and lower on lower.

Nostrils and ears: they start as a membrane across the opening and then go onto the skin, so nothing is left as a flap.

07The face on the body rig

Apply builds 843 facial joints, the MetaHuman control board and 739 corrective shapes. Weights and shapes then move onto the body mesh, with no seam at the neck.

A human face knows nothing about gums, tusks or an orc’s eyes. Three guards are stored on the rig as measured fixes; no geometry is edited.

Teeth guard: gums and teeth that come through the lip are set back.

Tusk guard: the lower lip stays in front of the tusk roots, the upper lip behind the tusks.

Eye guard: the lids stay on the eyeball in blinks, squints and frowns.

08Expressions

A set of expressions keyed on the board controls, eased into each other.

09The face board, control by control

Every board control is taken to each end of its range and measured against the neutral face. 149 of 156 control ends move something. Each tile is one control at one end.

Worth knowing: the nose wrinkle is the nose control moved up. nose_wrinkleUpper does nothing alone and only adds on top of it.

10Face from video

The reference is a video of a man making faces at the camera (Ketut Subiyanto on Pexels). MediaPipe reads 52 face weights per frame, and they map almost one to one onto the board.

The capture underplays, so each weight is rescaled to the performer’s own range in the clip.

11Still open

In the full angry face a speck of the eye shows under the lower lid.

The mouth control pushed fully sideways leaves the tusks behind.

The ear has a seam where the rim meets the head.

Side bends crease at the waist.

Not done yet: body mocap, Unreal export and a simulated loincloth.
